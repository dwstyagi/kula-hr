A payslip is the employer's monthly statement of three things: what the employee earned, what was taken out, and what was paid. That is the whole document.

The test of a good one is simple. Can an employee take the payslip, add up the earnings, subtract the deductions, and arrive at the number in their bank account — without asking anyone? If not, something is missing or mislabelled.

## The structure

Every payslip has two halves and a result:

**Earnings** — everything payable this month.
**Deductions** — everything withheld from those earnings.
**Net pay** — earnings minus deductions.

Everything else on the document is identification or context.

## Earnings, line by line

**Basic salary.** The foundation of the structure, usually 40–50% of the total. It matters beyond its own line because Provident Fund is calculated on it.

**House Rent Allowance.** Commonly a percentage of Basic. It also carries a tax exemption for employees paying rent under the old regime, which is why it is shown separately rather than folded into a single allowance.

**Special allowance.** Usually the balancing figure — whatever remains after the other components are set. It moves when anything else changes.

**Conveyance, medical and other fixed allowances.** Structure-dependent, and increasingly folded into special allowance.

**Variable pay.** Overtime, incentives, or a performance component for the month.

**Arrears.** Amounts relating to earlier months — typically a salary revision applied retrospectively, or a correction that could not be made to a payroll run already paid.

## Deductions, line by line

**Employee Provident Fund.** Generally 12% of Basic plus DA, subject to the statutory wage ceiling. Only the employee's share belongs here.

**Employee ESI.** 0.75% of gross, and only where gross falls within the ESI ceiling.

**Professional Tax.** A state levy, deducted by slab. Some states do not levy it, so a blank line here is not necessarily an error.

**TDS.** Income tax deducted at source, based on the employee's projected annual liability rather than a percentage of the month's pay. This is the line most likely to change month to month, and the [TDS guide](/blog/tds-on-salary) explains why.

**Other recoveries.** Salary advances, loan instalments, notice-period recovery or asset recoveries — anything agreed separately with the employer.

### The line that should not be there

**Employer PF and employer ESI do not belong in the deductions column.** They are company costs paid on the employee's behalf, not amounts withheld from earnings. Listing them as deductions makes net pay appear to reconcile incorrectly and is a common source of employee queries.

If you show them at all, show them in a separate employer-contribution section, clearly outside the deduction total.

## A worked example

An employee on ₹6,00,000 annual CTC, in a state levying ₹200 Professional Tax, with two LOP days in a 30-day month:

Monthly CTC is ₹50,000. Employer PF of ₹1,800 comes out of that first, leaving ₹48,200 of gross salary for a full month. Basic is 40% of monthly CTC, HRA is 50% of Basic, and special allowance takes the balance. Two LOP days in a 30-day month prorate the earnings to 28/30:

| Earnings | Amount | Deductions | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | ₹18,667 | Employee PF | ₹1,800 |
| HRA | ₹9,333 | Professional Tax | ₹200 |
| Special allowance | ₹16,987 | TDS | ₹0 |
| **Gross earnings** | **₹44,987** | **Total deductions** | **₹2,000** |

**Net pay: ₹42,987**

Two things in that example are worth noticing. The earnings are already reduced for the two LOP days — which is why a payslip must state the LOP count, or the figures cannot be checked. And employee PF is ₹1,800 rather than 12% of the Basic shown, because the contribution base is capped at the ₹15,000 statutory wage ceiling. ## What makes a payslip verifiable

Beyond the two columns, four things turn a payslip from informative into checkable:

**The pay period, stated explicitly.** Month and year, not just an issue date.

**Days paid and LOP days.** Without these, prorated earnings cannot be reconciled.

**Year-to-date figures** for gross, PF, Professional Tax and TDS. These are what employees need at tax-filing time, and what prevents twelve separate requests each March.

**Identifiers.** Employee code, PAN, UAN and bank account (usually masked). These are what make the document acceptable to a bank or a visa office.

## Issuing payslips

Two practical points that have nothing to do with the format.

**Publish, do not email.** Payslips sent as email attachments generate a permanent stream of "can you resend" requests, and password-protected PDFs generate a second stream about the password. A self-service portal where employees pull their own history — twelve months plus year-to-date — removes both.

**Issue them from the payroll run that produced them.** A payslip generated separately from the run it belongs to can drift out of step with what was actually paid and deposited. If a correction is needed after payment, it belongs in the next month as an arrear or recovery rather than as an edit to a payslip already issued.
